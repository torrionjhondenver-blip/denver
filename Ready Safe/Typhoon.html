🌀 Typhoon Preparedness

Be Prepared. Stay Safe. Protect What Matters.

🌧️ What Is a Typhoon?

A typhoon is a powerful tropical storm that forms over warm ocean waters. It can bring strong winds, heavy rain, flooding, and landslides. In the Philippines, typhoons can affect homes, schools, roads, and entire communities.

We cannot stop a typhoon from happening, but we can prepare for it. Knowing what to do before, during, and after a storm can help protect you and your family.

🏠 Before a Typhoon

Preparation is one of the best ways to keep your family safe. Do not wait until strong winds and heavy rain arrive.

  • Prepare an emergency kit. Keep drinking water, ready-to-eat food, medicines, a flashlight, batteries, a first-aid kit, and a power bank.
  • Monitor weather updates. Listen to PAGASA forecasts and warnings so you know when your area may be affected.
  • Secure your home. Bring loose outdoor items inside and close windows and doors. Only secure roofs or other structures before dangerous winds begin.
  • Charge your devices. Fully charge your phones and power banks in case electricity becomes unavailable.
  • Protect important documents. Place identification cards, certificates, and other important papers in waterproof packaging.
  • Plan your evacuation. Know your evacuation route and the nearest designated evacuation center. Leave early if authorities advise it.
  • Prepare your family. Discuss where to meet and how to contact one another if family members become separated.

🌪️ During a Typhoon

When a typhoon arrives, your safety is more important than your belongings. Stay informed and avoid unnecessary travel during dangerous weather.

  • Stay indoors. Keep away from windows and glass doors, where flying objects or broken glass may cause injuries.
  • Follow evacuation orders. If local authorities tell you to evacuate, move to the designated safe place as instructed.
  • Avoid floodwater. Never walk, swim, or drive through flooded roads because water can be deeper and faster than it appears.
  • Stay away from power lines. Fallen wires may still carry electricity. Report them to the proper authorities.
  • Listen to official announcements. Use a battery-powered radio or a charged phone to receive updates when possible.
  • Do not go outside during a brief calm. The eye of a typhoon can bring temporarily calmer conditions, followed by dangerous winds.
  • Save your phone battery. Use your phone for important communication and emergency updates when power is limited.

🏡 After a Typhoon

Even when the rain stops, danger may remain. Floodwater, damaged buildings, fallen trees, and electrical hazards can make returning home unsafe.

  • Wait for official advice. Return home only when authorities say it is safe.
  • Check your surroundings. Stay away from damaged buildings, unstable trees, and fallen power lines.
  • Use safe drinking water. Boil or treat water according to official health guidance if its safety is uncertain.
  • Check your food. Throw away food that has touched floodwater or has spoiled because of a power outage.
  • Clean up carefully. Wear protective footwear and gloves. Avoid touching wet electrical equipment.
  • Check on others. If it is safe, contact family members, neighbors, older adults, and people who may need assistance.
  • Report damage. Inform local authorities about dangerous conditions in your neighborhood.

💙 A Reminder for You and Your Family

Typhoons can be frightening, especially when strong winds shake your home or the power suddenly goes out. Feeling worried is normal. Being prepared can help you make safer decisions when the weather becomes severe.

Talk with your family before a storm arrives. Check on children and older family members, prepare your emergency supplies, and agree on where to evacuate if needed. Help your neighbors when you can do so safely.

Remember: Follow official warnings, evacuate when instructed, and never risk your life to save belongings. Your safety always comes first.
