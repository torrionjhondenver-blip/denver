🌍 Earthquake Preparedness

Be Prepared. Stay Safe. Protect What Matters.

🌎 What Is an Earthquake?

An earthquake happens when the ground suddenly shakes because rocks beneath the Earth's surface move. Sometimes the shaking is weak, but strong earthquakes can damage buildings, roads, and other structures.

Earthquakes can happen without much warning, which is why preparation is important. Knowing what to do can help you stay calm, make safer decisions, and protect yourself and the people around you.

🏠 Before an Earthquake

Preparing ahead of time can make a frightening situation easier to handle. You do not need expensive equipment to get started. Small steps at home and school can make a difference.

  • Prepare an emergency kit. Include drinking water, ready-to-eat food, a flashlight, a first-aid kit, necessary medicines, a power bank, and a battery-powered radio if available.
  • Make your home safer. Secure heavy shelves, cabinets, televisions, and other objects that could fall during shaking.
  • Identify safe places. Find sturdy tables or desks you can take cover under. Stay away from windows, glass, and tall furniture that could fall.
  • Make a family emergency plan. Decide where your family will meet after an earthquake and how you will contact one another if separated.
  • Know your exits. Learn safe ways to leave your home or school after the shaking stops. Keep pathways clear.
  • Practice what to do. Practice Drop, Cover, and Hold On with your family or classmates so everyone knows how to respond.

⚠️ During an Earthquake

The ground may shake suddenly, and it is natural to feel scared. Try to protect yourself first. Do not rush toward the exit while the shaking is happening.

  • Drop, Cover, and Hold On. Drop onto your hands and knees, cover your head and neck under a sturdy table or desk, and hold on until the shaking stops.
  • Stay away from windows. Glass can break, and objects may fall from shelves. Protect your head and neck.
  • If you are indoors, stay indoors. Do not run outside during the shaking. Stay under cover until it is safe to move.
  • If you are outdoors, move to an open area. Stay away from buildings, trees, streetlights, and power lines if you can do so safely.
  • If you are in a vehicle, stop safely. Stay inside the vehicle until the shaking stops. Avoid stopping under bridges, overpasses, or near power lines.
  • Do not use elevators. If you are in a building, protect yourself where you are and use the stairs only after shaking stops and it is safe to evacuate.

🏡 After an Earthquake

Even after the shaking stops, hazards may remain. There may be aftershocks, damaged structures, broken glass, or electrical dangers. Take your time and follow official instructions.

  • Check for injuries. Check yourself and people nearby. Give first aid only within your ability and call emergency services if someone needs urgent help.
  • Be ready for aftershocks. More shaking may occur. If it does, Drop, Cover, and Hold On again.
  • Leave damaged buildings carefully. If a building is damaged or unsafe, move away when it is safe to evacuate. Do not go back inside until authorities say it is safe.
  • Stay away from fallen wires. Never touch power lines or electrical equipment that may be damaged.
  • Check for possible hazards. If you smell gas or suspect a leak, leave the area and contact the appropriate emergency service. Do not use flames or operate electrical switches near a suspected gas leak.
  • Listen to reliable updates. Follow announcements from local authorities and PHIVOLCS. If you are near the coast and feel strong or long earthquake shaking, move to higher ground or inland immediately because a tsunami may be possible.

💙 A Reminder for You and Your Family

An earthquake can happen when we least expect it, and feeling afraid is completely normal. What matters is knowing how to respond and helping one another stay safe.

Talk with your family about your emergency plan, check on children and older family members, and make sure everyone knows where to go. You do not have to prepare everything in one day. Start with a few important steps and improve your plan little by little.

Remember: Stay calm when possible, protect your head and neck, follow official instructions, and never put yourself in danger trying to rescue belongings.
