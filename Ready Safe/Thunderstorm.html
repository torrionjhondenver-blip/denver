⛈️ Thunderstorm Preparedness

Be Prepared. Stay Safe. Protect What Matters.

⛈️ What Is a Thunderstorm?

A thunderstorm is a weather event that produces thunder and lightning. It may also bring heavy rain, strong winds, and sometimes hail. Thunderstorms can happen suddenly and may cause flooding, falling branches, power outages, and damage to homes or buildings.

In the Philippines, thunderstorms are common, especially during warm and rainy weather. Understanding the dangers and knowing what to do can help keep you and your family safe.

🏠 Before a Thunderstorm

Preparing before a storm arrives can help you avoid unnecessary risks. Pay attention to weather updates and prepare your home and family.

  • Check weather forecasts. Follow official thunderstorm and rainfall advisories from PAGASA.
  • Prepare an emergency kit. Keep drinking water, ready-to-eat food, a flashlight, a first-aid kit, essential medicines, and a power bank.
  • Secure outdoor objects. Bring loose items indoors so strong winds do not blow them away.
  • Check your home. Close windows and doors, and check for roof damage when conditions are safe.
  • Charge your devices. Charge your phone and power bank in case electricity is interrupted.
  • Plan to stay indoors. Avoid unnecessary travel if thunderstorms are expected.
  • Know your evacuation options. If your area is prone to flooding, know where to go when local officials issue an evacuation warning.

🚨 During a Thunderstorm

If you hear thunder, lightning may be close enough to strike. Go inside a substantial building or an enclosed vehicle with a hard roof as soon as possible.

  • Stay indoors. Keep away from windows and doors during strong winds and lightning.
  • Avoid open areas. Do not stay in fields, on hilltops, or in other exposed places during lightning.
  • Stay away from water. Avoid swimming, bathing, and other contact with water during a thunderstorm.
  • Avoid electrical equipment. Do not handle plugged-in appliances during lightning. If possible, unplug sensitive equipment before the storm arrives, not during it.
  • Never shelter under an isolated tree. Trees can attract lightning, and branches may fall during strong winds.
  • If you are driving, slow down and find a safe place to stop away from trees, floodwater, and power lines. Never drive through flooded roads.
  • Watch for flooding. Move to higher ground if water rises and follow official evacuation instructions.

⚡ Lightning Safety

Lightning is dangerous because it can strike people, trees, buildings, and other objects. You do not need to be standing in the rain to be at risk.

  • Go inside a substantial building or enclosed hard-top vehicle.
  • Avoid open fields, beaches, rooftops, and mountain peaks.
  • Stay away from metal fences and isolated trees.
  • Wait at least 30 minutes after the last thunder before returning outdoors.
  • If someone is struck by lightning, call emergency services immediately. It is safe to touch the person to provide assistance.

🏡 After a Thunderstorm

Even after the rain stops, hazards may remain. Fallen trees, damaged electrical wires, and flooded roads can still be dangerous.

  • Wait for the storm to pass. Do not go outside until it is safe and lightning has stopped for at least 30 minutes.
  • Avoid fallen power lines. Stay far away and report them to the appropriate authorities.
  • Check your home safely. Look for roof damage, broken windows, and water entering the house. Do not enter unsafe areas.
  • Avoid floodwater. Floodwater can hide open drains, sharp objects, and electrical hazards.
  • Check on your family. Contact family members and neighbors when it is safe to do so.
  • Follow official updates. Listen to local authorities for warnings and instructions about damaged roads or evacuation areas.

💡 Important Thunderstorm Safety Reminders

Remember: When thunder roars, go indoors. A covered outdoor waiting area, tent, or open shelter does not provide reliable protection from lightning.

Never walk or drive through floodwater. If water starts entering your home, move to a safer, higher location when possible and follow evacuation orders.

Your safety is more important than your belongings. Stay informed, avoid dangerous areas, and follow instructions from emergency responders.

💙 A Reminder for You and Your Family

Thunder, lightning, and strong winds can be frightening, especially when the electricity goes out or heavy rain continues for a long time. It is normal to feel worried. Having a plan can help everyone feel more prepared.

Talk with your family about where to shelter, keep emergency supplies in an accessible place, and make sure everyone knows what to do if flooding occurs. Check on children and older family members.

Remember: Do not take risks just because the rain seems lighter. Wait for the storm to pass and follow official safety advice before going outside.
