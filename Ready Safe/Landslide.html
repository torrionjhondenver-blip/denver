⛰️ Landslide Disaster Preparedness

Be Prepared. Stay Safe. Protect What Matters.

⛰️ What Is a Landslide?

A landslide happens when soil, rocks, mud, or other materials move down a slope. It can happen suddenly or develop slowly over time. Heavy rainfall, earthquakes, erosion, and unstable ground can trigger landslides.

Landslides can damage houses, roads, and buildings. Communities near steep hills, mountains, or areas with unstable slopes may be at greater risk. Understanding the warning signs and preparing early can help save lives.

🏠 Before a Landslide

If you live near a hill or slope, pay attention to weather updates and changes in the ground, especially during long periods of heavy rain.

  • Know your area's risk. Ask local officials whether your home or community is near a landslide-prone area.
  • Watch for warning signs. Look for new cracks in the ground, leaning trees or posts, and unusual changes in slopes.
  • Prepare an emergency bag. Include drinking water, food, a flashlight, a first-aid kit, essential medicines, and a charged power bank.
  • Make an evacuation plan. Know where to go and which safe routes to use. Follow evacuation guidance from local authorities.
  • Stay informed. Listen to weather bulletins and announcements from PAGASA and your local disaster office.
  • Plan for your family. Make sure children, older family members, and people who need assistance know how to evacuate.

⚠️ Warning Signs of a Possible Landslide

A landslide may happen with little warning. Recognizing unusual changes around you is important.

  • New cracks appearing in the ground, roads, or walls.
  • Trees, fences, or utility poles suddenly leaning.
  • Water appearing in unusual places on a slope.
  • Small rocks or soil beginning to fall from a hillside.
  • Unusual rumbling sounds or sudden changes in water flow.
  • Official warnings or evacuation instructions for your area.

These signs do not always mean a landslide will happen, but they should be taken seriously. Do not approach an unstable slope to investigate.

🚨 During a Landslide

If a landslide is happening or authorities warn you to evacuate, act quickly. Do not wait to collect personal belongings.

  • Move away from the path. If safe to do so, move away from the landslide and toward stable ground, following local emergency instructions.
  • Leave immediately when instructed. Follow designated evacuation routes and avoid crossing areas where soil or rocks are moving.
  • Stay away from slopes. Do not go near the landslide to watch, take photos, or retrieve belongings.
  • Avoid river channels. Mud and debris may move through valleys and waterways, even away from the original slope.
  • Watch for additional slides. One landslide may be followed by more, especially during heavy rainfall.
  • Help without putting yourself at risk. Alert emergency responders if someone is missing or trapped. Do not enter an unstable area to attempt a rescue.

🏡 After a Landslide

Even when the ground appears quiet, the area may remain dangerous. More soil or rocks may move, and roads or buildings may be damaged.

  • Stay away from the affected area. Do not return until authorities confirm it is safe.
  • Check for injuries. Get medical help for anyone who is hurt.
  • Avoid fallen power lines. Never touch damaged electrical equipment or wires.
  • Watch for more danger. Continued rain can trigger additional landslides or flooding.
  • Report hazards. Tell local authorities about damaged roads, buildings, or dangerous ground conditions.
  • Follow official announcements. Use reliable sources for information about evacuation centers and returning home.

💡 Important Landslide Safety Reminders

Remember: Heavy or continuous rainfall can increase landslide danger, especially near steep slopes. If officials tell you to evacuate, leave promptly and follow their instructions.

Never cross a moving landslide, walk over fresh debris, or drive through an affected road. The ground may still be unstable, even after movement stops.

Your safety comes first. Do not risk your life to save belongings or return to a dangerous area.

💙 A Reminder for You and Your Family

Landslides can be frightening, especially when heavy rain continues for hours. It is normal to feel worried, but knowing the warning signs and having a plan can help you make safer decisions.

Talk with your family about where to evacuate and how to contact one another. Check on neighbors who may need assistance, but never put yourself in danger while helping others.

Remember: Do not ignore warning signs. Early evacuation can save lives. It is always safer to follow official advice than to wait until the danger becomes obvious.
